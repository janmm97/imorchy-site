This DPA applies to hosted Orchy (app.imorchy.dev) when One Systems, Inc. ("Orchy", the "Processor") processes personal data for a customer (the "Customer", the "Controller") under the Terms of Use. It is part of those Terms. If the two conflict on personal data, this DPA wins. It does not apply to the desktop app, which sends us no data.

1. Definitions

"Data Protection Law" means the GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, the California Consumer Privacy Act and its regulations, and other privacy laws that apply to the Customer Personal Data. "Customer Personal Data" means personal data the Customer puts into hosted Orchy. "Sub-processor" means a company we use to process it. "Security Incident" means a breach of security that leads to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, or unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, Customer Personal Data. Other terms (controller, processor, personal data, processing, data subject) have their GDPR meanings.

2. Roles

The Customer is the controller (or, if it acts for another, a processor) and Orchy is its processor. For account, sign-in and waiting-list data (the Privacy Policy, sections 3.1 and 3.2), Orchy is an independent controller and this DPA does not apply.

4. What Orchy does and doesn't do

Orchy will:

  1. process Customer Personal Data only on the Customer's documented instructions, which are the Terms, this DPA and the Customer's use of hosted Orchy. If the law requires other processing, we tell the Customer first unless the law forbids it (Art. 28(3)(a));
  2. make sure everyone who handles it is bound by confidentiality (Art. 28(3)(b));
  3. apply the security measures in Annex 2 (Art. 28(3)(c), Art. 32);
  4. follow section 6 on sub-processors (Art. 28(3)(d));
  5. help the Customer answer data-subject requests (section 7) (Art. 28(3)(e));
  6. help the Customer with security, breach notices, impact assessments and consultation with regulators, taking into account what we know (Art. 28(3)(f));
  7. delete or return the data at the end (section 11) (Art. 28(3)(g));
  8. give the Customer the information needed to show we follow this DPA, and allow audits (section 10) (Art. 28(3)(h)). We tell the Customer at once if we think an instruction breaks Data Protection Law.

5. The Customer's duties

The Customer must have a legal basis for the data it puts in, give any notices required, and make sure its instructions follow the law. The Customer chooses its AI provider and connected apps. A provider the Customer connects with its own key is the Customer's own vendor, not our Sub-processor; data the Customer's agents send to it is governed by the Customer's terms with that provider.

6. Sub-processors

The Customer gives general authorisation to the Sub-processors on our Data Privacy page (Annex 3). We bind each by written terms that give the same level of protection as this DPA, and we stay responsible for their work. We give the Customer at least 30 days' notice by email before adding or replacing one. The Customer may object on reasonable data-protection grounds within that time. If we can't resolve it, the Customer may stop using hosted Orchy and delete its data (section 11) without penalty.

7. Data-subject requests

If a data subject contacts us about Customer Personal Data, we pass it to the Customer and don't answer it ourselves unless the law requires. We give the Customer reasonable help to find, correct or delete data in its Orchy. The Customer can download files from its Orchy's file viewer.

8. Security Incidents

We tell the Customer without undue delay, and within 72 hours after we become aware of a Security Incident. We say what we know: what happened, what data, what we have done, and what we recommend. We keep the Customer updated and help it meet its own notice duties. A notice isn't an admission of fault.

9. Transfers

Customer Personal Data is stored on a server in Canada (OVH, Beauharnois) with encrypted backups at Cloudflare in Eastern North America, and some Sub-processors are in the US (Annex 3). Where Data Protection Law restricts a transfer, we rely on an adequacy decision or on appropriate safeguards (GDPR Art. 46), such as the standard contractual clauses. For restricted transfers from the EU, the parties agree to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Module Two, Customer to Orchy as controller to processor; Module Three for onward transfers to Sub-processors) and, for the UK, the UK addendum. The European Commission recognises Canada as adequate for commercial organisations. For our US Sub-processors we rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (and its UK extension) where the Sub-processor is certified, or on standard contractual clauses in its data processing terms.

10. Audits and records

On reasonable written request, no more than once a year (or after a Security Incident), we give the Customer the information needed to show we follow this DPA, such as answers to a security questionnaire. If that is not enough, the Customer may audit us, at its cost, on reasonable notice during business hours, under confidentiality and without access to other customers' data. We hold no SOC 2, ISO 27001 or similar certification.

11. Deletion and return

When the Customer clicks "Delete my Orchy", or its access is removed, we delete its files, published copies and email, including the copy set aside on our server during deletion, within 30 days. Its final encrypted backup is removed 14 days after it was made, as every backup is. Before deleting, the Customer can download files from its Orchy's file viewer. We may keep data only where the law requires, and then protect it under this DPA.

12. Liability

Each party's liability under this DPA is subject to the limits in the Terms (the greater of what the Customer paid in the last 12 months or US$100), to the extent the law allows. Nothing limits a data subject's rights under Data Protection Law.

13. Term and order of precedence

This DPA lasts as long as we process Customer Personal Data. If it conflicts with the Terms, this DPA wins on personal data. Where the SCCs apply, they win over this DPA. California law governs, except where Data Protection Law or the SCCs require otherwise.

14. Health and special-category data

Hosted Orchy is not designed for health records or patient data (HIPAA), children's data, or other special-category data (GDPR Art. 9). The Customer must not put health or patient data into Orchy, and must not put in other special-category or children's data without our written agreement. We don't sign business associate agreements (BAAs).

15. California

For the CCPA, Orchy is a service provider. We don't sell or share Customer Personal Data, don't use it outside our direct business relationship with the Customer or for any purpose other than providing hosted Orchy, don't combine it with other data except as the law allows, and will tell the Customer if we can no longer meet these duties.

Annex 2: Security measures

Only measures we can show are in place are listed.

  • Each customer has its own container on our server (<slug>.imorchy.dev); access passes an authentication check before it reaches the container.
  • Web traffic reaches our server only through a Cloudflare Tunnel. The server's firewall admits nothing else from the internet except administrative access, which is by key-only SSH with password and root login turned off.
  • Sign-in by Google or one-time email link; sessions use a random value of which only a hash is stored; the cookie is HttpOnly and Secure and lasts 30 days.
  • The Customer's own AI-provider key is sealed with AES-256-GCM, using a key derived from a server-side secret that is separate for each customer.
  • Backups are encrypted (with the open-source tool age) before upload to Cloudflare R2; copies older than 14 days are removed.
  • Connections to the service use HTTPS. Encryption in transit ends at Cloudflare's edge, and traffic is carried to our server over the Cloudflare Tunnel.
  • The run log for connected apps keeps no message bodies and is kept for 90 days.
  • Each customer's agents run as a separate unprivileged user with no system privileges.
  • A customer's container cannot reach the server itself or private networks.
  • Each sign-in cookie is limited to its own address (our app and each customer's Orchy get separate host-only cookies), and every signed-in change needs an anti-forgery token and must come from Orchy's own page (the browser's Origin is checked).
  • A customer's Orchy won't open until the current Terms are accepted.

Annex 3: Approved Sub-processors

See the table on the Data Privacy page. In short: Cloudflare, Inc. (site, tunnel, encrypted backups; US company, backups in Eastern North America), OVH (server; Canada), Resend, Inc.
