# What changes the cost besides the hourly rate

The hourly rate is the number everyone quotes. Four other things move the bill:

- **Hours.** Few people need exactly 40 hours a week. Agencies often sell monthly retainers or hour buckets, and part-time needs can still cost a retainer minimum.
- **Skills.** A general VA, a bookkeeper and a developer sit at very different points on the same table.
- **Time zone and location.** Geography is the biggest lever on the hourly rate, which is why the Philippines and the US rows above are so far apart.
- **Hiring and management time.** A direct hire means you recruit, onboard and manage. An agency does that for you, and the price reflects it.

## How the numbers were built

- **Tasks and AI cost** are measured. The AI cost includes every agent run, failed runs and hand-offs among them, plus the agents' own setup runs and the orchestrator's routing and reporting.
- **Hours** are estimates. Each completed task was given the time a capable, already-trained person would need to do it by hand.
- **Hire-out cost** multiplies those hours by hourly rates for each hiring option. The hourly rates are our estimates for each type of work, based on 2025 and 2026 published ranges and BLS wages where they exist. They are not quotes and don't map one to one onto the tables above.
- **The AI cost** is priced at pay-as-you-go API rates, not the amount a business was billed.
- **Business C** covers only 3 days, so its figures are a short sample, not a month.

# What this comparison leaves out

> **What this leaves out.** Orchy's own price, the owner's setup time, and any subscription or API plan costs. The time the owner spent giving instructions and reviewing drafts. The value of a person's judgment on work an agent can't do. The recruiting and onboarding a hire needs.

The assumptions run both ways:

- Nobody hires an assistant for 149 scattered hours. A real hire often means a salary, a retainer or an agency minimum, plus recruiting and onboarding time. That would push the hire-out figures up.
- The per-task times are estimates for a trained person. A different person, or a different way of working, would change them in either direction.
- Owner time exists with a human hire too, so leaving it out affects both sides.
- Three businesses over a few weeks, one of them over 3 days, is a small sample. It shows how the comparison works. It does not predict what you would see.

# Frequently asked questions

## How much is a VA per hour?

It depends on where and how you hire. Published vendor ranges run from $5.00 to $11.50 for a direct Philippines hire, an effective $12 to $19 through a managed offshore agency, and $18 to $35 on a freelance platform. BLS has no separate category for virtual assistants; the closest, executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants, has a US median wage of $36.82 an hour, before employer costs.

## How much does a virtual assistant make?

BLS has no separate category for virtual assistants. The closest is executive secretaries and executive administrative assistants, with a May 2025 US median wage of $36.82 an hour. In the Philippines, HireTalent.ph lists $5.00 to $11.50 an hour by experience level.

## What is the average monthly salary for a virtual assistant in the Philippines?

HireTalent.ph's 2026 guide lists $880 to $1,056 a month for beginners, $1,144 to $1,408 for mid-level and $1,496 to $2,024 or more for senior and specialist assistants. Managed full-time VAs through an agency run $1,988 to $3,000 a month, according to MyOutDesk.
