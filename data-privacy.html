Data Privacy: DPA and sub-processors

Last updated: November 4, 2026 · Version 1.0

This page is for businesses that use Orchy and need to know who processes their data and on what terms. Orchy is a product of One Systems, Inc.

1. Today: Orchy does not process your data

The Orchy app runs on your own computer. Your data (tasks, files, client data, logs, keys) stays there, and Orchy does not receive it. That makes you the controller, and Orchy is not your processor. So no data processing agreement (DPA) is needed for the app.

When you connect outside services (your AI engine, a routing provider, One, ElevenLabs, Deepgram, Cloudflare), you deal with them directly, under your own accounts and their own terms and DPAs. For the Orchy app, they are your vendors, not Orchy's sub-processors.

Access calls: if we help you set up Orchy on a call and see your data, we use it only to help you and don't keep copies. We don't record access calls unless we ask you at the start of the call and you agree.

2. When a hosted plan launches

We plan a hosted version of Orchy. It is not live. When it launches, Orchy will process customer data for you, and we will offer a DPA before you put any data in it. That DPA will:

  • be based on the Common Paper Data Processing Agreement standard (v1.1), with its attribution;
  • cover what Article 28 of the GDPR requires: processing only on your instructions, confidentiality, security measures, sub-processor rules (notice of changes and a right to object), help with data-subject requests and breaches, deletion or return at the end, and information for audits;
  • list approved sub-processors on this page.

We will update this page, and tell existing customers in advance, before adding any sub-processor.

Common Paper Data Processing Agreement (v1.1) is free to use under CC BY 4.0, https://commonpaper.com/standards/data-processing-agreement/.
